# Build brief: story 2.3, planner action view, open items and provenance

Silurian Assay. Sprint 2, third and closing story.

No new fixture. Fixtures 30 and 31 are already on `main` with their expectations, and both carry every case this story needs.

Story 2.1 made the portfolio legible. Story 2.2 recorded a decision per line. **This story turns a decision into an action a planner can take, and captures the answer when they take it.** When it closes, sprint 2 closes.

---

## 1. What this story is

Three things:

1. The **do this** half of the drawer, filling the slot 2.1 deliberately left empty.
2. The **open items list**: every line whose refusal is unresolved or deferred, ranked so the client knows what to work on first.
3. **Resolution capture**, which 2.2 deferred: the planner picks a code from the closed vocabulary and the run records it.

Plus provenance, which is small: every statement on a line can show which stage produced it.

**Still no forecasting.** No method runs in sprint 2 at all. If anything calls a model, scope has leaked.

---

## 2. The action text, which is the substance of this story

Every decision gets a **do this** written in the language of a planner's next move, not the language of the tool. The **so what** from 2.1 explains why the line is the way it is. The **do this** says what to do about it, and the two must not repeat each other.

| Decision | The action |
|---|---|
| `model_eligible` | Nothing to decide. This line goes into the forecast comparison, and the value comes from the accuracy work in sprint 3. |
| `model_eligible_wide_interval` | This is a buffer conversation, not a plan number. The point forecast will be wrong most months and that is expected. Set the safety stock from the interval and the service level you have promised, and stop chasing the mean. |
| `intermittent_methods` | An order-cycle conversation. Ask the customer how they actually order, then decide whether a min-max, a call-off schedule or a consignment arrangement fits better than a monthly forecast. |
| `policy_only` | A customer conversation about order patterns and lead time. No forecast will fix this line. The options are a stocking agreement with committed volumes, make to order with an agreed lead time, or carrying the buffer knowingly and pricing it. |
| `insufficient_evidence` | A scoping decision. Either supply more history, forecast it by analogue to a comparable line, or take it out of scope for this engagement and say so. |
| `refused_data_quality` | A data request, and name the specific one: more periods, a corrected extract, or confirmation that this line is genuinely new. |
| `discontinued_confirm_status` | A status question for the business. If it is finished, it should also come out of the master data and the stock holding, which is usually where the money is. |

Two rules for that column. **Numbers before adjectives**, and no hedging. And **no method names beyond the decision families**: the tool says intermittent methods, not "use Croston with an SBA correction", because sprint 3 owns which method actually runs.

The `policy_only` and `model_eligible_wide_interval` rows are the two a client is most likely to argue with, so they carry the most weight. Both say the same uncomfortable thing in different ways: a better forecast is not the answer here, and the honest move is to change the arrangement or accept the buffer.

## 3. What a resolution does, and what it must not do

This is the decision that shapes the story, and I want it built exactly as written.

**A resolution records an answer and changes scope membership. It never changes a routing decision by assertion.**

| Resolution | Effect |
|---|---|
| `DISCONTINUED_CONFIRMED` | Line marked out of forecast scope, resolved, off the open items list. Flagged for obsolescence and master data review. |
| `SUPERSEDED_BY_SKU` | Line marked out of scope, resolved, with the successor SKU recorded as a link. **History chaining is not built here.** Recording the relationship is enough for this story. |
| `STILL_ACTIVE_DEMAND_GAP` | Recorded and resolved. The decision does not change, and the interface says why: the line may well be alive, but this extract still cannot support a forecast for it. |
| `STILL_ACTIVE_DATA_MISSING` | Recorded and resolved, flagged as requiring a new extract. The decision does not change in this run. |
| `SUPPLY_LONGER_HISTORY`, `SUPPLY_CORRECTED_EXTRACT` | Recorded as a data request. The decision does not change in this run. |
| `TREAT_AS_NEW_LINE` | Line marked as a launch line, out of statistical scope, resolved. Sprint 3 decides what a launch route actually does. |
| `EXCLUDE_FROM_SCOPE` | Line out of scope, resolved. |
| `DEFER` | Nothing changes. The line stays on the open items list. |

**Why decisions do not move on an assertion.** A routing outcome that changes because someone said something, rather than because the data changed, cannot be defended in a readout. The client's own planner would be able to talk any line into eligibility, and the audit trail would show a forecast built on a claim. Where a resolution genuinely should change the answer, the honest route is a new run with corrected data, and the interface should say that plainly rather than pretending the answer moved.

**This is the same principle as resolution not override from story 1.1.** The user supplies information the tool did not have. They do not overrule a finding.

Every resolution produces a **new pass** recorded in the manifest options: the code, the SKU, the timestamp and, for `SUPERSEDED_BY_SKU`, the successor. The free-text note is client data and goes in the bundle only.

## 4. The open items list

Every line with an unresolved or deferred refusal, in one place. This is a client deliverable in its own right and the first thing in this tool that a consultant would email.

- **Ranked by volume share descending.** This is where ABC finally earns its keep. The decision never depended on volume; the running order does.
- Each row: SKU, volume share, decision, the reason, and the resolution options available.
- A count and a volume total at the top: **so many lines, so much of your volume, waiting on you.**
- Empty state says every refusal is resolved and names when.

On fixture 31 this list opens with five lines carrying 34.43 percent of volume, which is the sentence that makes the tool worth paying for.

---

## 5. Provenance

Small, and it closes the 2.0 structure work.

Every statement on a line can show which stage produced it: validation, quality, classification or routing. A tooltip, a small label, an expandable row, your call, but it must be available without leaving the line.

The reason is the 1.6 lesson made visible. When a client asks why two numbers on one screen disagree, the answer should be readable from the screen rather than reconstructed by a consultant.

---

## 6. Interface

Follows the 2.0 structure. Self-contained panel components, tokens from `index.html`, no new colours, no second typeface, state encoded twice on every row.

- The **drawer** gains the do this block, and the resolution picker on refused lines. The picker is a closed list, never a free-text field. A note field may sit beside it and is optional.
- The **open items list** is its own panel, reachable from the routing panel and from the run context.
- Selecting a resolution shows what will happen before it is applied, in one sentence, taken from the table in section 3.
- Grid, filter, sort and search state survive a panel switch, as before.

## 7. The closing test for sprint 2

Story 2.1 tested whether a planner could **find** the A-volume lumpy line inside a minute. This story tests whether they can say what to **do** about it.

Same conditions: one person who did not build it, no narration, the screen alone. Ask what they would do first, and about which line.

**Pass:** they name PKG-50301 or RTG-60301, and their answer is a conversation with the customer about order patterns, not a better forecast or a different model.

**Fail:** they propose tuning a model, or they cannot say what the next step is.

Every number can be correct and this test can still fail, which is why it is stated separately. It is also the last acceptance criterion in sprint 2, so the sprint does not close until a person who is not you has passed it.

## 8. Acceptance

1. Every decision carries a do this that matches the substance in section 2, in the house register, with no method names beyond the decision families.
2. The do this and the so what say different things on every decision. No sentence appears in both.
3. Resolutions are picked from the closed vocabulary. There is no free-text path to setting a resolution anywhere in the interface.
4. Each resolution has exactly the effect in the table in section 3, and no resolution changes a routing decision or a quality band.
5. Selecting a resolution shows its consequence before it is applied.
6. Every applied resolution produces a new pass recorded in the manifest options, with code, SKU and timestamp, and the successor SKU where the code is `SUPERSEDED_BY_SKU`. No free-text note reaches the manifest.
7. The open items list shows every unresolved or deferred refusal, ranked by volume share descending, with a count and a volume total at the top. On fixture 31 it opens with five lines and 34.43 percent of volume.
8. `DEFER` leaves the line on the open items list and changes nothing else.
9. The empty state names when the last refusal was resolved rather than showing an empty table.
10. Every statement on a line can show which stage produced it, without leaving the line.
11. Both fixtures still produce their recorded results. Fixtures 30 and 31 and all three expectations files still hash to their recorded values.
12. The 1.6 cross-stage tests still pass, and no new finding code is introduced by this story.
13. Each new panel renders in isolation in a test.
14. Manifest and bundle schemas bumped one minor if their shape changed, with both numbers stated in the pull request, and no client data in the manifest.
15. Scoped string check, production only: no "nine box", no "value" adjacent to ABC.
16. The one minute action test in section 7, run on one person who did not build it. **Sprint 2 does not close until this passes.**
