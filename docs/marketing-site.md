# Silurian marketing site: intent and conventions

Recorded: 4 September 2026. Last amended: 6 September 2026.

This file preserves material that existed only in the ChatGPT Codex session that built the marketing site. That session is closed. Nothing in the original entry changed the site, and no code or copy was touched to write it. That stopped being the whole story on 6 September 2026: the file is now also the site's running record, and any entry carrying a later date was written in the same commit as the change it describes.

It has two parts. Section one is intent as stated by the product owner, which is the least recoverable material and explains why the site is as it is. Section two is the conventions and traps a successor will not infer from the files. A third section records three facts that live outside this repository or outside `main`.

The site itself is `index.html` and `privacy.html` at the repository root. Process for changing it stays in `MAINTENANCE.md`, and release evidence stays in `PROJECT_HANDOFF.md`.

## 1. Intent, as stated by the product owner

- **The site is an identity page for the limited company.** It exists so the company can be found and verified. It is not intended to grow into an elaborate sales site, and a change that pushes it that way should be questioned before it is built.
- **The Bedrock artwork was the visual inspiration.** The faceted stone, the flat orange field and the paper ground come from it.
- **Rejected treatments.** Two approaches to the Assay link were considered and turned down: the large logo side treatments, and the neutral product card. What was wanted was a restrained dropdown in the header beside the company name. That is what shipped in pull request 67. The dropdown itself was then removed on 4 September 2026, on preference and not on any defect, once the product owner had seen it live. The header then carried a plain text link reading AI Demand Forecasting, pointing at `forecast-risk.html` so that a public visitor lands on a page explaining the offer rather than on the Assay password gate. Both halves of that have since moved: on 7 September 2026 the label became AI Forecast Diagnostic and the destination became `forecastability.html`. Assay is still not reached from the header.
- **The hero stone was removed on 6 September 2026.** On the product owner's instruction and on redundancy, not on a defect: the same mark already sits in the header, the footer and the favicon, so the page showed it three times and once at 471px. The art is kept as an asset at `assets/hero-stone.svg`, referenced by nothing, because a service image is under consideration for that slot. Removing it takes the last inline SVG off the homepage.
- **Private company information does not go on the website.** It goes to SharePoint or a similar controlled store. Do not add a website backend to hold it.
- **Email harvesting mitigation was discussed and deliberately deferred.** The contact address is a plain `mailto:` link and is therefore machine readable. That is a known, accepted position, not an oversight.
- **The LinkedIn banner experiments were abandoned.** They are not pending work.

## 2. Conventions and traps for a successor

Each point below was checked against the files on 4 September 2026, and the points dated 6 September 2026 were rechecked on that date.

- **CSS authority.** Colour and the font declaration come from `tokens.css` at the repository root, linked by every page. Everything else, all layout, sits in the `<style>` blocks embedded in each page. `index.html` was the colour authority until 4 September 2026 and is no longer: it is the authority for its own layout only. Two questions, then, before changing a value: which file owns it, and, once inside that file, which declaration wins. The root once also held `styles.css` and `ds-styles.css`, linked by nothing, so editing one appeared to do nothing; both were removed on 4 September 2026 with the rest of the Claude Design export.

  There is a second authority rule inside a file, and it is not the same rule. The first is that the file you are reading may not be the authority. The second is that **the declaration you are reading may not be the one that wins.** This now cuts both ways: a page that still defines its own `:root` value overrides `tokens.css`, because the page's `<style>` block comes after the link. A page is only truly on the tokens once its own definitions are gone. `index.html` holds three `<style>` blocks and defines several tokens more than once, and the last definition is the live one. Finding a token is not the same as finding its value: read every definition of it, then confirm against a rendered pixel.

  What that cost when ignored: `--color-divider` was `color-mix(in srgb, #3f3d3b 40%, transparent)` in one `:root` block and plain `#3f3d3b` in a later one, so every rule on the site was drawn solid and not at 40 percent. A change made from the first definition looked correct in the diff and rendered a header seam at `#b2b1b1` against the homepage's `#3f3d3b`. Only the pixels showed it.

  **That trap is now closed rather than merely documented, and closing it is what the band was for.** `--color-divider` and `--color-accent` each have exactly one definition, in `tokens.css`. No page declares either. The losing 40 percent value is out of the tree entirely and survives only as the open question recorded below. Among the mechanics of the band, this is the part that mattered: a value can no longer be read from one declaration and rendered from another.

  The orphaned stylesheets that this convention used to warn about are gone. `styles.css` and `ds-styles.css`, byte-identical copies carrying a red `#ec3013` accent and `#201e1d` text, were removed on 4 September 2026 along with ten other Claude Design export files. **The reason for removing them was not the dead CSS.** It was `readme.md`, which instructed a future developer in the imperative and in the house voice to link `styles.css` from every page and take every colour from its variables. Following it would have reapplied the abandoned palette and reintroduced the Google Fonts request removed the same morning, while reading as the rule rather than the mistake. `_ds_manifest.json` did the quiet version, declaring the red accent as the token set. A dead stylesheet does nothing; a document telling someone to use it undoes the work. That is the shape to watch for: the dangerous residue of an abandoned approach is its documentation, not its code.
- **Structure.** In order: header, `#about` headline and subline at full content width, a 2px seam, then a lower band with the paragraph left and three service domains right, the orange `#contact` section, footer. The `#about` section was a two-column split with the copy left and the hero stone right until 6 September 2026. The stone went first, leaving one column; the design session then set the current shape, which is not the same thing as the intermediate state and should not be mistaken for it. `privacy.html` is a separate page with its own embedded styles.
- **Logo duplication, mostly closed.** There were two separate representations of the mark until 6 September 2026, the inline hero SVG in `index.html` and `logo-stone.svg`. The hero is gone, so every mark the site renders is now `logo-stone.svg`, used by the header, the footer and the favicon, and one change reaches all three. The hero art survives as `assets/hero-stone.svg` and is not loaded by any page, so it will not follow a change to `logo-stone.svg`. If it is ever put back on a page, the duplication returns with it.
- **Mobile.** Below 720px the hero logo used to be hidden on purpose, which is why removing it changed nothing at 390 and 320px: both screenshots are byte identical across the change. The service lists collapse to a single column at the same width, and the header navigation may wrap beneath the company name on narrow screens. That wrap is accepted behaviour.
- **The font is checked by test, and the check earns its place because this failure is silent.** `SelfHostedFontLoads` in the marketing control asserts that `tokens.css` declares exactly one `@font-face` source, that it is root relative, that it is not a third-party origin, and that the file it names exists in the repository at a plausible size. A root-relative URL plus `font-display: swap` means a broken path paints a fallback and looks deliberate, which is what happened to this project's design mocks and was caught by eye rather than by any tool. Proved by moving `assets/fonts/Archivo-Variable.ttf` aside and watching the suite fail on it, then putting it back, as well as by three in-test probes for a broken path, a relative form and a Google Fonts URL. It does not prove a browser loaded the face; only a browser can.
- **Typography.** Archivo is self hosted from `assets/fonts/Archivo-Variable.ttf` and its SIL Open Font Licence is retained at `assets/fonts/OFL.txt`. Keep both. The header link is set at 15.5px to match the real service text on the page, not at the 12px used in the mockup. The mockup is not the reference for size. That 15.5px carried over from the dropdown it replaced.
- **The site token band buys prevention, not repair.** Anyone comparing screenshots from before and after this work will see no difference, and that is the point. There was no live second palette by the time the band started: the pages already agreed on every value they shared. What they did not share was a source. Three pages used three names for the same role, `--color-bg` and `--bg` and `--paper`, with nothing connecting them, which is how `forecast-risk.html` ran a second palette in Production for weeks without anyone seeing it. The band removes the mechanism, not a visible fault. Judge it by whether drift can return silently, not by whether the site looks better.
- **There is a type scale since 6 September 2026, all three pages are on it, and it is enforced.** Seven steps live in `tokens.css` as `--text-poster`, `--text-display`, `--text-title`, `--text-subhead`, `--text-lead`, `--text-body` and `--text-label`, set by the design session. They are extracted rather than invented: every step is a size already in use. No page carries a hand-written size, and `MarketingSiteTypeScale` fails the build if one appears, with no allowance file, exactly as the colour control works. **The check reads the `font` shorthand as well as the longhand**, because the header link's 15.5px was hiding in a shorthand where a `font-size` scan did not see it.

  **The rule for a fluid size, which is the whole lesson of this band: a `clamp()` is classified by what it renders at desktop, never by the number typed at the small end.** The minimum is a mobile floor, not a role. Ignoring that produced four wrong numbers across two documents: 42px for a headline rendering 57.6px, 56px folded into a 31px step, and two secondary page headlines recorded as absent when they render 76px and 68px. All three page headlines now take the 40px display step. Poster at 56px is spent only on a closing accent field, never on a page opener.

  **The wordmark is outside the scale rather than an exception to it.** `--wordmark-size` holds its 15 to 18 taper, which no step matches. A token is not a hand-written size, so the control has nothing to permit and the scale keeps no exceptions.

  **Four values keep their own `clamp()` and are held for a decision**, because no step fits their rendered range. The `forecast-risk.html` headline renders 76px and the `privacy.html` headline 68px, so the 40px display step would cut them by 47 and 41 per cent; the 56px poster step would cut them by 26 and 18 per cent and is the closer fit. The header wordmark tapers 15 to 18, which no step matches: the 19px lead step would grow it four pixels on a phone.

  The fourth, `forecast-risk.html`'s stat value, is the mild one and an earlier version of this note overstated it. It renders 40px, which is the display step exactly, so that mapping costs nothing at desktop and four pixels on a phone. It is held only because its declared 26px minimum is what the design's table maps, and that reading would take it to 25px. Two readings of one value, and they differ by 15px.

  **The scale's claims did not survive contact with the rendered page, three times, and always the same way.** The brief's inventory read fixed `font-size` values and skipped every `clamp()`. It recorded the headline as 42px when the live headline measured 57.6px at 1440, so the 40px display step is a 30 percent reduction at desktop rather than a 2px snap. The same miss put the 56px closing line against a 31px title step; the design session answered that by adding the `poster` step at 56px, which is the right shape of fix. The third instance is the four held values above, found when the rollout reached the other two pages. **Measure a `clamp()` in a browser before believing any size inventory**, and note that a size can also hide inside the `font` shorthand, where a `font-size` scan will not see it: the header link's 15.5px was there.

  **A one pixel type change broke a layout, which is the argument against calling any type change cosmetic.** Moving the `forecast-risk.html` table from the 11px micro-label to the 12px step pushed the exceptions table 10px past a 320px viewport and the whole page scrolled sideways. The scale keeps no exceptions by decision, so the fix was the cell padding at narrow widths, not the size. Nothing in the source diff would have shown this. It was found by rendering all three pages at four widths and asserting that `scrollWidth` never exceeds `clientWidth`, which is worth doing after any type change.

  The superseded convention, kept because it explains the file's older comments: `index.html` uses 24 distinct raw font sizes, `forecast-risk.html` 15 and `privacy.html` 4, and only `15.5px` repeats at any scale. That is a list, not a scale. Tokenising it would mean inventing one, which is design work. The token file therefore carries colours and the font declaration, and the raw-value test pins colours only. A future band that wants a type scale is creating it, not extracting it.
- **The logo colours cannot have one source.** The mark is six raw values, `#2b2a29`, `#3f3d3b`, `#1a1918`, `#111010`, `#c15613` and `#ec6917`. An external SVG loaded through `<img>` cannot read the page's custom properties, so those six cannot be tokenised without changing how the mark is embedded, which is a restructure. The raw-value test excludes SVG `fill` attributes for this reason and not by oversight.

  Since 6 September 2026 no page holds any of them: they live in `logo-stone.svg` and in `assets/hero-stone.svg`, and `index.html` carries no `fill` attribute at all. **The exclusion was deleted with the hero, in the same change and not afterwards.** It was not set to zero. Nothing is stripped from a page before the colour scan runs any more, so an inline `fill` is now an ordinary raw colour and fails the first assertion in the control, and `test_an_inline_svg_fill_is_a_finding` states that in the suite.

  **The exclusion was weaker than it looked, and the deletion is a tightening rather than a tidy.** It pinned the number of fills at six and stripped their values before the scan, so it could see a seventh fill appear and could not see any of the six change. Proved rather than argued: the pre-removal page was restored with one logo fill moved from `#2b2a29` to `#ff0000`, and the old control passed all ten tests on it. The new one fails on the same page. A red value could have entered the mark on the homepage and the build would have stayed green.

  Two of the six never painted a solid pixel. `#2b2a29` was a base polygon fully covered by the three drawn over it, and `#111010` was a sliver falling almost entirely outside the mark's own clip path. Both were repainted bright green and magenta and the render compared: zero pixels of either, and 700 differing pixels, every one an anti-aliased edge blend. They are edge softening, not areas. Worth knowing before anyone tries to match the mark's palette from its source.
- **Open question, the 40 percent divider.** `--color-divider` is defined twice, and the losing definition is `color-mix(in srgb, #3f3d3b 40%, transparent)`. Nobody now knows whether a 40 percent hairline was the original intent and the solid override an accident. The site has shipped solid since launch and the product owner has approved that appearance repeatedly, so solid is what the token file carries. The question is recorded for a later band and is not a defect.
- **The round contact badges are the site's one agreed radius exception.** The email and LinkedIn badges in the contact band are circular against a zero-radius system. The design session raised it as a defect on 6 September 2026 and withdrew it the same day: James agreed the round badges before design was involved and they are not up for revision. Nothing else on the site carries a radius. Do not raise it a third time.
- **The header brand block is one link on every page.** The mark and the wordmark sit inside a single anchor to `index.html`, so clicking either goes home. Before 6 September 2026 the two pages disagreed and each was half right: `privacy.html` linked the wordmark and not the mark, `forecast-risk.html` linked the mark and not the wordmark. Both are now the single-link pattern, verified by clicking each half in a browser and confirming where it lands. `index.html` is the exception and is deliberate for now: its brand block is not a link at all, because it is the home page, and whether it should self-link is with the design session.
- **A grid child count is a layout constraint, and it fails silently.** Adding the sub-note under the homepage paragraph put a third child into a two-column grid, which pushed the services list into the left column beneath the paragraph. No test failed, no overflow appeared, and the page still rendered. It was caught by measuring element positions rather than by looking at a screenshot thumbnail. The paragraph and its sub-note are now one wrapped grid child. **Count the children before adding one to a grid with a fixed template.**
- **Open question, the contact badges are not Archivo.** The circular email and LinkedIn badges in the contact band set `font-family: Arial, sans-serif` at weight 700. They are the only elements on the site not in Archivo. Left untouched under the pixel-identical rule and recorded for the product owner.
- **`tokens.css` is the one source for colour and the font.** Every colour the site renders and the `@font-face` declaration live in `tokens.css` at the repository root, linked by all three pages. Layout CSS stays embedded in each page, deliberately: this was a token extraction, not a restructure. The file landed inert on 4 September 2026, linked but overridden by each page's own values, and the pages adopt it one at a time.
- **Surfaces sit on both sides of the ground.** `--color-surface` `#eae9e9` is darker than the `#f3f2f2` page and `--color-paper` `#fff` is lighter. The darker panel is deliberate and recorded in `CLAUDE.md` section 9. The natural assumption is that a surface is lighter than its page, and here that is only half true.
- **`--color-paper` carries two roles on one value.** A surface lifted off the page ground, such as the masthead band and the chart box, and type or marks reversed out of a filled accent or ink field. One token rather than two, because the system is print derived and both are bare paper. The name is not an invention: the design system named the role itself, in `index.html`, as "type reversed to the paper token". If the surface white ever moves off pure white the token splits then, and that split is cheap when it is actually needed.
- **A probe proving a control is broken does not prove what the breakage would have cost.** The fourth-page guard was genuinely blind to an unstaged page, and the probe proved it. The conclusion drawn from that, that such a page would be live in Production, was not established and was wrong: Vercel builds from the commit, so the guard would have caught the page in CI before any deploy. The fix was right, the finding was right, and the consequence attached to it was overstated. Keep the two apart when writing a finding up, because a correct fix carried a false claim into three documents on the strength of a real probe result.
- **A cleanup recorded as complete when it was partial.** S1.1a removed 18 comments that had been left describing removed CSS, and was recorded as having swept them. It had not. The detector only looked for a comment followed by another comment or by the end of a block, so three orphans sitting among surviving declarations went straight through it and were found a story later, in S1.3. The cause was the detector's shape, not carelessness, which is exactly why the record needed correcting: a cleanup described as complete when it was partial is the same class of fault as a document describing a state it no longer has, and the next person trusts it the same way.
- **The colour control lives in the Assay suite, on purpose, and belongs to the marketing session.** `forecast-app/tests/test_marketing_site_tokens.py` tests the marketing site, not the application around it. Ownership moved on 6 September 2026, and the directory did not: the file is the marketing session's to change, everything else under `forecast-app/` is not. The reason is that the hero removal put the page and the control on opposite sides of a session boundary, so whichever half landed first would have left `main` red. A control that can only be changed by a session that cannot change the thing it controls is not a control, it is a deadlock. `forecast-app/tests/` is the only suite anything invokes, and a control that does not fire is worse than one filed in the wrong drawer. The cross-product status check has no other home in any case, since it spans both products. The file carries the same note in its header. Do not delete it as misplaced; if the marketing site ever gains its own invoked suite, move it and delete the note with it.
- **The control pins colours and not type.** Every colour on the three pages must come from `tokens.css`, in any form: hex, `rgb()`, `hsl()`, inside a `color-mix()`, or as a canvas literal. Font sizes and weights are deliberately not pinned, because 24 distinct sizes with no scale behind them means a test asserting a list, which would fail on every legitimate edit. A control people learn to ignore is worse than no control.
- **The site has four pages since 7 September 2026.** `forecastability.html` is the Assay argument page, and the header link now points at it rather than at `forecast-risk.html`, which becomes the page a reader arrives at from there. The header label reads AI Forecast Diagnostic, the design session's settled wording, approved by the product owner on 7 September 2026 and applied the same day. It read AI Demand Forecasting from 4 September until then. The new page carries no hand-written size and no raw colour, and it was added to `EXPECTED_PAGES` only after both controls passed on it, which is the order the note below requires.
- **A new page must fail the control, and that is the point.** The page list is pinned at four, and was pinned at three until 7 September 2026. Do not widen it to let a new page through: a new page is exactly when a second palette appears, which is how `forecast-risk.html` came to run the export's colours in Production. Put the new page on the tokens first, then add it to the list. Pages are read from the filesystem rather than from Git so the control fires as soon as a page exists, instead of waiting for it to be staged. That is the whole reason: earlier failure, no dependence on staging discipline. An unstaged page is not deployed, because Vercel's Git integration builds from the commit, so the gap the filesystem read closes is a local one, not a public one. The exception is a manual `vercel --prod` from a working directory, which does upload untracked files; that is not how this project is documented to deploy.
- **The verification did not miss the control defect. It endorsed it.** That is the reason this note exists, and it is worse than a simple gap. Every visual check in this repository has been four viewport widths in headless Chromium, which reproduces layout and colour faithfully and does not reproduce platform control styling at all. A native control that does not state its own `color` takes the platform's default, and **the default of the browser doing the checking happened to be the right-looking one**: Chromium supplies black, near enough to the page ink to read as correct, where iOS supplies the system blue. So the Run sample analysis button was not merely unexamined for a week. It was examined repeatedly, at four widths, and passed every time, because the tool and the defect shared the same blind spot. A checker whose own default matches the intended appearance cannot see a missing declaration.

  The same blind spot covers iOS rounding control corners against the zero-radius rule, platform focus rings, and control fonts. **Only a check on a real device answers these.** Treat a clean desktop pass on anything involving `button`, `select`, `input` or `textarea` as unproven until someone opens it on a phone.
- **Every control states its own colour, and native controls neutralise the platform appearance.** `color` is declared explicitly on every interactive control rather than left to inheritance or a browser default, and native form controls carry `appearance: none` where nothing is lost by it. The exception is the `select` on `forecast-risk.html`, which keeps `appearance: auto` deliberately: neutralising it removes the dropdown arrow on every platform, which is the only thing telling a reader the control opens a list. That was tested, not assumed. A functional loss is not worth a cosmetic risk.
- **Auditing colour.** A hex scan is not a colour scan. The same value can be written as a hex, as `rgb()` or `rgba()`, inside a `color-mix()`, or as a canvas literal in script, and a search for `#ee7623` finds none of the others. Correcting this page's accent meant changing two hex occurrences and two `rgba(238,118,35, ...)` occurrences that a hex scan had already reported as clean. Sample the rendered pixels to confirm a colour change, on both the page and, where a canvas is involved, after the drawing has run.
- **Status colours are a cross-product decision.** `forecast-risk.html` uses `#356b46` good, `#9a5a12` warn and `#a52a1f` bad. These three have no equivalent in the site token set, they appear nowhere in the Claude Design export, and Assay uses the same three values in `forecast-app/static/index.html`. They are therefore a deliberate choice shared across both products, not a stray import. Changing them on one side without the other splits the pair, so decide both together and record it. The four remaining unmapped values on this page, `#66615f` muted, `#77716e` inventory line, `#d1cdca` gridlines and `#e3dfdd` reversed copy, are likewise held by decision on 4 September 2026 and are not oversights.
- **Cardiff in the meta description is deliberate.** The description says Cardiff, Wales while the footer's registered office is Pontypridd. Confirmed by the product owner on 6 September 2026 as his general location, against the registered office being the legal address. Do not raise it again as an inconsistency, and do not change one to match the other.
- **A broken character sat in the meta description for months, and the diagnosis matters more than the fix.** It rendered as `Silurian Consulting LTD â€” project management`. The file is valid UTF-8 and declares its charset, so neither was the cause: the stored bytes were themselves a double-encoded em dash, `c3 a2 e2 82 ac e2 80 9d`, produced by reading UTF-8 as latin-1 and saving again. It is one of 39 such sequences in `index.html`; the other 38 are inside CSS comments and never reach a reader, which is why only this one was visible. Replaced with a comma on 6 September 2026, satisfying the house dash rule at the same time. **If a character looks wrong on a page, check the bytes before touching the charset declaration.** The remaining comment sequences are recorded, not fixed, because nothing renders them.
- **The homepage speaks in the first person plural, as the firm.** "We bring twenty years of experience..." and "Tell us what needs to land", set by the design session on 6 September 2026, replacing a page that mixed third person, first person plural and the passive. It went through first person singular in between, for about an hour, and was reversed: "me" was built and then withdrawn, so a reader of the branch history will find both. `forecast-risk.html` and Assay keep a product voice on purpose, being about a product rather than about the firm.
- **The homepage runs no JavaScript.** `index.html` contains no `<script>` of any kind, since the header dropdown that needed one was removed on 4 September 2026. That is worth keeping. The page needs no framework, no analytics and no interaction handler to do its job, and every script added is something a visitor downloads and a reviewer has to reason about. Add one deliberately, with a stated reason, or not at all.
- **Deployment.** The marketing site deploys from the Vercel project `silurian-site`: repository root, framework preset Other, no build command, production branch `main`. Assay deploys separately from `forecast-app/` through the project `silurian-forecast-diagnostic`. A pull request therefore shows two Vercel checks. The second is expected and is not an accidental duplicate. Confirm the check that belongs to the component you changed.
- **Preview verification.** An anonymous request to a Vercel preview URL can return the Vercel login page with HTTP 200. A 200 alone does not prove the preview loaded. Check the returned content, not the status code. Verify previews in a signed in browser.
- **Every handoff between the design session and this one is a committed Markdown file, and that is a standing instruction from the product owner rather than a habit.** He routes every message himself and neither session can see the other's chat, so an exchange that lives only in a chat window is not a record and cannot be audited later. The design session's brief is `docs/designdecisions.md`, kept verbatim including anything this session found to be wrong; corrections go in this file and in `PROJECT_HANDOFF.md` rather than being edited into someone else's document. Replies from this session are `docs/build-response-N-<date>.md`, numbered in order. Questions in them take the house format from `CLAUDE.md` section 1: what it blocks, the recommended default, the cost if wrong. Write the reply as a file first and hand that across, not a summary in chat.
- **Source of truth is GitHub.** Not a local checkout, and not a design export. Transfer intended changes into a branch rather than overwriting the repository with an export.

## 3. Facts that are not visible in a clone

### Archived branch, kept deliberately

Branch `archive/story-1-2-local-5e994d5`, at commit `5e994d55a279bc2d5d935eb28bf42d4c522f63a6`, holds a commit that existed only on the product owner's laptop. It was pushed under a new name during the Codex shutdown because the original branch had diverged. It is unreviewed, it is unmerged, and its content has not been reconciled against `main`.

It is kept on purpose. Do not merge it, do not delete it, and do not treat it as stale housekeeping.

### Two files that exist outside Git

Two files live on the product owner's laptop under a Codex session folder and are not in this repository. They will not follow a clone and are not recoverable from GitHub:

- `assay-link-options.html`, the dropdown mockup.
- `check-assay-header.cjs`, a browser check script for the header.

They are recorded here so that a reference to either is recognised rather than searched for.
