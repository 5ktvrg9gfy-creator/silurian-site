## Why a new fixture was needed

Ten of the twelve lines in `20_portfolio_mixed.csv` classify as smooth. The portfolio classification matrix would render nearly empty and routing would have almost nothing to choose between.

The distribution here:

| class | SKUs | share of volume |
|---|---|---|
| smooth | 5 | 67.06% |
| lumpy | 3 | 17.73% |
| erratic | 2 | 9.13% |
| intermittent | 3 | 5.43% |
| unclassifiable | 2 | 0.66% |

That shape is realistic. Most pharma packaging portfolios earn most of their volume from a handful of well behaved lines, and the interesting money sits in the tail.

## The planted finding

**PKG-50301 is lumpy, third by volume at 13.86 percent, and ABC class A.**

This is the finding the portfolio classification matrix exists to produce. A client looking at the grid should see, inside a minute, that a material line will not forecast well under any statistical method, and that the answer is a conversation with the customer about order patterns rather than a better model.

If the screen renders correctly and a planner still cannot find that line quickly, the presentation has failed even though every number is right. Worth holding the 2.1 UI against that specific test.

Two supporting cases:

- **PKG-50201**, intermittent, 5 percent of volume. Orders every third month at a consistent size. Routing must treat it differently from PKG-50301 despite both being sporadic. That is the entire reason ADI and CV squared are separate axes.
- **PKG-50102**, erratic, class C. Demand every period, size swinging by an order of magnitude. It tests that the grid does not bury non-A problems.

## Boundary and refusal cases

| SKU | tests | expected |
|---|---|---|
| PKG-50401 | both cuts from the smooth side, ADI 1.21, CV² 0.383 | smooth |
| PKG-50402 | both cuts from the lumpy side, ADI 1.46, CV² 0.601 | lumpy |
| PKG-50501 | exactly 3 non-zero observations, the minimum | intermittent, CV² is a number |
| PKG-50502 | 2 non-zero observations, one below the minimum | unclassifiable, CV² null |
| PKG-50602 | a single observation | unclassifiable, CV² null |
| PKG-50601 | 10 periods, short but complete | smooth |

PKG-50401 and PKG-50402 are the ones that move if the comparison operators are wrong-sided or if the sample estimator is used instead of the population estimator. PKG-50502 and PKG-50602 are the 1.2 defect in fixture form: a CV² of 0.0 on a one observation series would classify it as smooth, which is the most confident possible answer about the least evidence in the file.

PKG-50601 makes a separate point. **Classification refuses on observation count, not on history length.** Ten periods is short, and the quality stage already says so. Classification still has enough to answer, and duplicating the history caveat here would recreate exactly the cross-stage contradiction 1.6 deleted.

## Unclassifiable is a fifth state

Not a nulled smooth, and not an error. It needs a cell on the grid, a written label and a plain sentence saying why: too few observations to describe a pattern. Two of fifteen lines land there, carrying 0.66 percent of volume, so it is cheap to show honestly.

## The XYZ problem, and how it was settled

XYZ measures the coefficient of variation across **all** periods, including zeros. On an intermittent or lumpy line the coefficient is driven by how often demand is absent rather than by how stable it is when it arrives.

PKG-50201 shows it: orders every third month at a near constant 2600 units, which is about as predictable as an intermittent line gets, yet CV across all periods is 1.386 and it lands in Z. The letter says volatile. The data says clockwork.

So the expectations file carries an `xyz_meaningful` flag, true only for smooth and erratic lines. Seven of the fifteen qualify: five smooth and two erratic. The JSON has carried 7 throughout; the count of five in my covering note was wrong.

**Settled: the primary matrix is ABC crossed with demand class, not ABC crossed with XYZ.** This is not a nine box. Five demand states including unclassifiable, crossed with three ABC volume classes, gives 15 possible cells, of which 11 are populated in this fixture. It is called the portfolio classification matrix, and that name is used in the interface and in every document from here. XYZ stays as a detail field on the lines where it is meaningful, and reads "Not meaningful for this demand class" where it is not. Never blank.

The textbook grid is ABC by XYZ, and a planner may expect to see it. The counter is that half the cells in it would be artefacts of the zeros, and a client who works that out has reason to doubt everything else on the page.

The `abc_by_class_cells` block in the JSON gives the eleven populated cells, and the four empty ones are as real a part of the matrix as the rest.

## The sprint 2 decisions, settled by the product owner

1. **Routing scope.** Routing belongs to story **2.2**, which records the decision, the reason and the refusal state without executing the selected method. Method implementation stays in sprint 3. Revision 1.0 of this note put routing in 2.1, which contradicted the build brief. The brief is correct: 2.1 classifies and segments, and emits no method name at all.
2. **ABC basis.** Cumulative volume until unit cost arrives at 4.1. Labelled **ABC volume class** everywhere. The word value does not appear.
3. **Five stories into three**, on three testable boundaries: 2.1 classification, ABC volume segmentation and the classification matrix; 2.2 routing decision, reason and refusal; 2.3 routing-aware planner action view and provenance.
4. **XYZ.** ABC crossed with demand class is the primary matrix, unclassifiable included as the fifth class. XYZ is shown only where `xyz_meaningful` is true and reads "Not meaningful for this demand class" everywhere else. Never blank.

The principal UI acceptance test for sprint 2 is PKG-50301: a planner must find the A-volume, lumpy, 13.86 percent line inside one minute and understand from the screen why customer engagement matters more than model selection.

That test splits across two stories. **2.1 must make the line findable**, tested by asking which line someone looks at first. **2.3 must make the action obvious**, tested by asking what they would do about it, which cannot be answered until routing exists.

## Method note to carry into the sprint 2 documentation

CV² uses the population estimator, dividing by n. The SBC literature's 0.49 cut is stated against the sample estimator, so values here sit systematically lower by a factor of (n-1)/n. Negligible at 35 periods, 20 percent at 5 observations.

The practical effect is that short intermittent lines lean towards intermittent rather than lumpy. That is the safer direction, since it routes to a review rather than to a model, but it should be written down in the method note rather than discovered later by someone comparing our output to a textbook.

1. A successful `/api/quality` run appends the classification stage and returns `classification_result`. Validation-only runs are unchanged.
2. Classification is its own tab, between Data quality and Forecast.
3. That panel owns the 15-cell matrix and the extended classification grid.
4. Quality bands and findings stay sourced from `quality_result`, joined on SKU for display, never duplicated inside `classification_result`.

Default 4 is why the `classification_must` block in the JSON says present rather than carry. One fact, one owner, and any bundle consumer performs the same join.
