## 1. Implementation defaults, set by the product owner

These are decided. Build to them, and raise a question only if one proves impossible rather than merely inconvenient.

1. **A successful `/api/quality` run appends the classification stage and returns `classification_result`.** Validation-only runs are unchanged. Classification never runs on a file that did not reach quality, which is what makes the manifest edge in section 6.1 always satisfiable.
2. **Classification gets its own tab, between Data quality and Forecast.** That position is the reading order of the argument: here is what the data is, here is what kind of demand it is, here is what a model can do with it.
3. **That panel owns the 15-cell matrix and the extended classification grid.**
4. **Quality bands and findings stay sourced from `quality_result`.** They are joined to the classification view on SKU for display and are **not** duplicated inside `classification_result`. One fact, one owner.

Default 4 is the one with a consequence worth stating. Any consumer of a bundle, including our own export, must perform the same join, so a classification result read on its own is deliberately incomplete: it says what kind of demand a line is and says nothing about whether the data is trustworthy. That is correct and it is the price of not having two copies of a band that can disagree. Make it explicit in the bundle's `method_note` so a client's analyst reading the file six months from now does not conclude the bands went missing.

## 2. What this story is

Every SKU that reaches this stage gets four things: a demand class, an ABC volume class, an XYZ letter where that letter means something, and a plain sentence saying what the combination implies. The portfolio gets a matrix that puts those lines in cells, and a work surface that lets a planner find the one that matters.

**This story does not choose a forecasting method and does not run one.** Routing is story 2.2 and it records a decision rather than executing it. Method implementation is sprint 3. If a method name appears anywhere in 2.1 output, scope has leaked.

Sprint 2 is three stories, not five:

| Story | Boundary |
|---|---|
| 2.1 | Classification, ABC volume segmentation and the classification matrix |
| 2.2 | Routing decision, reason and refusal state |
| 2.3 | Routing-aware planner action view and provenance |

The line between 2.1 and 2.3 is **what a planner can see against what a planner should do**. 2.1 makes the portfolio legible: the matrix, the class columns on the grid, and the what and so what halves of the drawer. 2.3 makes it actionable, once routing exists to say anything about action. A "do this" sentence in 2.1 would be a routing recommendation written before routing is built, which is how the two stories bleed into one.

The quality engine already computes ADI and CV squared per SKU, so most of 2.1 is applying thresholds to numbers that exist. Read section 4 before assuming otherwise, because the one thing that must not happen is a second implementation of those two metrics.

## 3. Names, fixed

These are product owner decisions and are not open:

- **Portfolio classification matrix.** Not a nine box. Five demand states crossed with three ABC volume classes is 15 possible cells, of which 11 are populated in the fixture. Do not call it a nine box in code, in comments, in copy or in the manifest.
- **ABC volume class.** ABC is computed on cumulative volume until unit cost arrives at story 4.1. The word **value** must not appear next to ABC anywhere in the interface, the export or the manifest. A client who reads value and receives volume has been misled, and it is their own cost data that fixes it.
- **Unclassifiable** is the fifth demand state. Not an error, not a null, not a nulled smooth. It occupies cells and appears in counts.
- The four Syntetos-Boylan-Croston states keep their standard names: smooth, erratic, intermittent, lumpy.

## 4. The calculation, and the rule that governs it

### 4.1 One implementation, not two

ADI and CV squared are computed **once**, in the quality engine, and classification consumes those values. It does not recompute them, not even identically.

This is the 1.6 rule generalised. Two implementations of one judgement agree on the day they are written and drift apart on the next threshold change. That defect has already cost this project one remediation story.

**Add to the permanent suite:** for every fixture and every SKU, assert **exact value equality** between the ADI and CV squared that classification reports and the values held in `quality_result` for that SKU. Exact means no tolerance and no rounding on either side of the comparison, including nulls, which must be null on both sides rather than one null and one zero.

Not object identity. Both results cross a JSON boundary, so identity is meaningless by the time anything can be compared. Value equality after serialisation is the property that actually matters, since that is the form the client receives.

If both sides read the same `quality_result` field the test passes trivially, which is the point. If it is hard to write, the metrics have been duplicated and that is the defect this test exists to find.

### 4.2 Thresholds

All values are in the `thresholds` block of `expected_classification.json`, which is authoritative. Restated here for review, not for copying by hand:

| Threshold | Value |
|---|---|
| `adi_cut` | 1.32 |
| `cv_squared_cut` | 0.49 |
| `cv_squared_estimator` | population, dividing by n |
| `cv_squared_min_nonzero_observations` | 3 |
| `cv_squared_basis` | non-zero periods only |
| `abc_basis` | volume |
| `abc_a_cumulative_pct` | 80 |
| `abc_b_cumulative_pct` | 95 |
| `xyz_basis` | CV across all periods including zeros |
| `xyz_x_max_cv` | 0.5 |
| `xyz_y_max_cv` | 1.0 |
| `xyz_min_periods_present` | 3 |

Comparisons are strictly below the cut for the lower band. ADI below 1.32 is smooth or erratic; ADI at or above 1.32 is intermittent or lumpy. CV squared below 0.49 is smooth or intermittent; at or above 0.49 is erratic or lumpy. PKG-50401 and PKG-50402 sit either side of both cuts specifically to catch a wrong-sided operator.

### 4.3 The refusal rule

Fewer than three non-zero observations means **CV squared is null and the class is unclassifiable**. Null, never 0.0.

A one-observation series has zero variance by arithmetic, so a naive implementation reports CV squared of 0.0 and classifies it smooth, which is the most confident possible statement about the least evidence in the file. That exact defect was found and fixed in 1.2. PKG-50502 and PKG-50602 exist to stop it coming back.

Refusal is on **observation count, not on history length**. PKG-50601 has ten periods, which is short, and the quality stage already says so. Classification still has enough to answer and returns smooth. Repeating the history caveat here would recreate the cross-stage contradiction that 1.6 deleted.

### 4.4 ABC volume class

Rank by total volume descending, ties broken by SKU ascending so the order is deterministic. Accumulate share, inclusive of the ranked line. A line whose cumulative share crosses a cut sits in the band it crosses into: at or below 80 is A, above 80 and at or below 95 is B, above 95 is C.

Accumulate **unrounded** shares and round only for display, so the final line reads exactly 100.00. My expectations file accumulated shares already rounded to two places, which diverges by at most 0.013 points and leaves its last cumulative reading 100.01. That is inside the stated 0.1 tolerance and is a flaw in my file rather than in yours. No line in the fixture sits closer than 0.98 points to either cut, so rounding cannot move a band.

### 4.5 XYZ and where it is honest

XYZ is the coefficient of variation across **all** periods including zeros. On an intermittent or lumpy line that coefficient is driven by how often demand is absent rather than by how stable it is when it arrives. PKG-50201 orders every third month at a near constant 2600 units, about as predictable as an intermittent line gets, and lands in Z.

So:

- `xyz_meaningful` is true only for smooth and erratic lines. Seven of the fifteen in the fixture: five smooth and two erratic.
- Where it is true, show the letter.
- Where it is false, show the string **"Not meaningful for this demand class"**. Not blank, not a dash, not "n/a". A blank cell reads as missing data and invites a client to ask what went wrong.
- Where `periods_present` is below 3, `expected_xyz` is null and the same string applies.

`xyz_meaningful` is a property of the demand class, not of the value. It is derived from the class and must not be computed any other way.

## 5. What the engine emits

### Per SKU

`first_period`, `last_period`, `periods_present`, `periods_expected_in_span`, `non_zero_periods`, `zero_periods`, `adi`, `cv_squared_nonzero`, `cv_all_periods`, `demand_class`, `volume_total`, `volume_share_pct`, `rank_by_volume`, `cumulative_volume_share_pct`, `abc_volume_class`, `xyz`, `xyz_meaningful`, and `unclassifiable_reason` where the class is unclassifiable.

Field names in `expected_classification.json` use `expected_` prefixes on the three assigned labels because it is a test file. Production names them `demand_class`, `abc_volume_class` and `xyz`. Map them in the harness rather than renaming either side.

### Per portfolio

`sku_count`, `volume_total`, `class_counts` across all five states, `volume_share_by_class_pct`, `abc_counts`, `xyz_meaningful_count`, and the populated cells of the matrix.

**Volume share by class is the number that sells the finding**, more than the counts are. Three lumpy SKUs out of fifteen sounds like a rounding error. Lumpy carrying 17.73 percent of volume is a meeting.

### Stage boundary, and what the fixture actually does

Classification describes. It emits no findings, raises no verdict and blocks nothing. The quality band already attached to each line is carried through and displayed, never re-derived. Under the 1.6 cross-stage test, no classification code may share a namespace with a validation or quality finding code.

**Correction to revision 1.0.** I wrote that the fixture produces zero quality findings. It does not, and I asserted it without running the engine. Run against the live engine at analysis date 2026-08-01 it validates as accept with no findings, and quality returns a **caveated** portfolio band, one portfolio finding and six findings across five SKUs:

| Subject | Finding | Why it is correct |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio | `LONG_TAIL_CONCENTRATION` | Lines beyond the 99 percent cumulative point. The long tail was built deliberately. |
| PKG-50101 | `OUTLIER_CANDIDATE` | Erratic line, large orders against a low median. |
| PKG-50102 | `OUTLIER_CANDIDATE` | Erratic line, same shape at smaller scale. |
| PKG-50301 | `OUTLIER_CANDIDATE` | The planted lumpy A-volume line. |
| PKG-50601 | `HISTORY_TOO_SHORT` | 10 periods against an 18 period threshold. |
| PKG-50602 | `HISTORY_TOO_SHORT`, `SERIES_DISCONTINUED` | One observation, last seen 2024-10. |

Every one of those is the engine behaving correctly on data built to have exactly those properties. **Do not weaken the engine to make the fixture pass.** `expected_classification.json` v1.2 records them in a `quality_interaction` block, which is marked observed rather than specified: the engine is authoritative, and if the engine changes the block is updated to match it.

Two things follow, and they are the more interesting half of this story.

**Classification presents the bands unchanged, and does not own them.** All 15 lines are classified regardless of band. Nothing is suppressed, softened, re-derived or annotated, and per default 4 nothing is copied: the band is joined from `quality_result` on SKU at display time. Whether a method may run on a caveated line is a routing decision belonging to 2.2.

**Three of the flagged lines are flagged for outliers, and all three are erratic or lumpy.** That is not a coincidence and it is not a defect in either stage. A modified z-score test flags the large orders on a genuinely sporadic series, and those spikes are the demand pattern rather than data errors. The smooth seasonal lines PKG-50001 and PKG-50002 are not flagged, which confirms deseasonalisation is working above the 24 period minimum.

So the two stages are saying complementary things about the same SKU: quality says these values look extreme, classification says the extremes are the shape of the demand. That is not the 1.6 contradiction, where two stages disagreed about one property. Showing them together in the drawer is better than either alone, and it is the recommended copy treatment. PKG-50602 is the check that the distinction holds: quality says the history is too short and the series is discontinued, classification says unclassifiable on observation count, and the two agree.

## 6. Manifest and bundle contract

Classification is a **manifest stage**, on the same footing as validation, quality and forecast. It is not a decoration on the quality stage.

### 6.1 The stage

- `stage: "classification"`, added to the stage enum.
- `input_ref` is the **quality stage's `output_ref`**. Classification consumes `quality_result` and nothing else. Assert the edge: `classification.input_ref.sha256` equals `quality.output_ref.sha256`. The manifest's integrity is verified as a dependency graph rather than by array position, so this is the edge that proves classification saw the quality output it claims to have read.
- `output_ref` points at `classification_result`.
- `options` carries every effective threshold from section 3.2 including defaults actually applied, plus `cv_squared_estimator`, the estimator note, and `abc_basis: "volume"` with a note that cost is not available until story 4.1.
- `outcome` carries **counts and codes only**: class counts across all five states, ABC counts, `xyz_meaningful_count`, and matrix cell occupancy. No SKU codes, no volumes, no shares.

That last point is not a detail. The manifest contains no client data, and a matrix cell occupancy map is a count per cell, while a list of which SKUs sit in a cell is client data. This is the same mistake I made at story 1.4, when the manifest carried the client's source filename and you caught it.

### 6.2 The bundle

`results.classification` becomes a permitted key, required whenever a classification stage appears in the manifest, and absent whenever it does not. Bundle results use `additionalProperties: false`, so this cannot be added by omission: the schema has to name it.

Required inside it: `per_sku` keyed by SKU with every field in section 5, `portfolio` with the aggregates, `matrix` with all 15 cells including the empty ones, `thresholds` as applied, and `method_note` carrying the estimator explanation in prose a client can read.

`results.classification` carries **no quality band and no quality finding**, per default 4. The bundle already holds `results.quality`, and a consumer joins the two on SKU. The `method_note` says so in one sentence, so an analyst opening the file later reads a deliberate design rather than a gap.

Add the conditional both ways. A manifest with a classification stage and a bundle without `results.classification` must fail validation, and so must the reverse. A bundle that claims a stage ran and cannot show its output is worse than one that never ran it.

### 6.3 Version bumps, both required

| Schema | From | To | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `run_manifest.schema.json` | 1.3 | **1.4** | New stage enum member and its conditional block |
| `run_bundle.schema.json` | 1.0 | **1.1** | New `results.classification` key and its two-way conditional |

Both carry `const` version strings, so a reader refuses a version it does not know rather than reading it partially. That rule is already stated in the bundle schema and it is why a bump is mandatory rather than optional: an old reader must fail loudly on a new bundle, not silently ignore a stage.

Say both bumps in the PR description. Story 1.4 shipped a schema change without a bump because I did not ask for one, and I would rather over-specify this than repeat it.

Golden files need regenerating for both schemas, and the golden manifest's hashes must verify under the production rule, which clears `manifest_sha256` alone before hashing so `content_fingerprint_sha256` covers the real value. I got that wrong once already.

## 7. Interface

Follows the 2.0 structure without exception. Every new output is a self-contained panel component that owns its heading and body and knows nothing about its siblings.

**The matrix panel.** Fifteen cells, three ABC volume classes by five demand states. Each populated cell shows the line count and the share of volume. Empty cells are drawn, not hidden, because an empty intermittent-A cell is a real statement about the portfolio. Selecting a cell filters the grid to those lines. The volume figure is the primary number in each cell, not the count.

**The grid** gains four columns: demand class, ABC volume class, XYZ, and volume share. It keeps every 2.0 behaviour: sort on any column, filter, search, and state survives a panel switch.

**The detail drawer** gains a classification block carrying the **what** and the **so what**, and stopping there. What: this line is lumpy, ADI 3.89, CV squared 0.99, 9 non-zero periods in 35, and the quality stage flags its largest orders as outlier candidates. So what: demand arrives sporadically and in sizes that vary by an order of magnitude, so no statistical method will forecast it reliably, and those flagged spikes are the pattern rather than errors in the file. Numbers before adjectives, and no hedging.

**The do this half is story 2.3**, because the action depends on routing and routing does not exist until 2.2. Writing it here means writing a method recommendation before the story that decides methods. The drawer is built with the slot present and empty, so 2.3 fills it rather than restructuring it.

Colour follows the 2.0 rule. Orange as a mark or a field, never a five-state palette. Demand class is written on every row. Removing colour entirely must leave the screen fully readable.

## 8. The principal acceptance test

**A planner who has not seen this portfolio must find PKG-50301 inside one minute and be able to say why it matters.**

It is lumpy, third by volume at 13.86 percent, ABC volume class A. A material line that no statistical method will forecast well, where the answer is engagement with the customer rather than a better model.

The test has two halves and they belong to different stories:

- **2.1 must make the line findable.** Give someone the screen, no narration, one minute, and ask which line they would look at first. If they land on a smooth A line, or on the two unclassifiable lines carrying 0.66 percent between them, the matrix is directing attention at the wrong thing and 2.1 is not done.
- **2.3 must make the action obvious.** Same person, same screen, asked what they would do about it. The answer wanted is a conversation with the customer about order patterns, not a better model. That half cannot be tested until routing exists, so it is the closing criterion for sprint 2 rather than for this story.

Every number can be correct and both halves can still fail, which is why they are stated separately from the numeric criteria.

The likely failure is that A-class lines dominate visually because they are large, and lumpy A is one cell among fifteen. The matrix must make the intersection findable, not merely present.
