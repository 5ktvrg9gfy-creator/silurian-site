# Routing table, version 1.2

Story 2.2. Version 1.1 added the discontinued exception you called. **Version 1.2 makes every refusal resolvable by a closed set of choices rather than by a sentence somebody types.** Section 3.1 is the new part.

Routing **records** a decision. It does not run a method. Methods arrive in sprint 3.

---

## 1. The seven decisions

A closed set. Every line gets exactly one.

| Decision | Meaning | Forecast eligible |
|---|---|---|
| `model_eligible` | Demand arrives every period and the size is stable. A model and the statistical baselines can both run and be compared. | Yes |
| `model_eligible_wide_interval` | Demand arrives every period but the size swings hard. A model can run, and the honest output is a range rather than a number. | Yes |
| `intermittent_methods` | Demand arrives with gaps but at a consistent size when it comes. This needs the Croston family, not a general model. | Yes |
| `policy_only` | Demand is both sporadic and variable. No statistical method will forecast this well. The answer is inventory policy and a conversation with the customer. | No |
| `insufficient_evidence` | Too few observations to describe a pattern at all. | No |
| `refused_data_quality` | The data is not usable, whatever the demand looks like. | No |
| `discontinued_confirm_status` | No demand for six periods or more. There is nothing to forecast forward until somebody says whether the line is alive. | No |

The distinction between `intermittent_methods` and `policy_only` is the entire reason ADI and CV squared are separate axes. Intermittent means gaps you can plan around. Lumpy means gaps and sizes you cannot.

**`discontinued_confirm_status`, added in 1.1.** Refusing a dead line on data quality would be the wrong sentence. The data is fine. What is missing is demand, and no amount of cleaning changes that.

It also has two readings and both are worth money. Either the product is finished and it is still carrying master data, safety stock and planner attention, or demand stopped and nobody raised it. A line that was thirteen percent of volume and went quiet for fourteen months is a finding in its own right, whichever of those it turns out to be. So the decision asks a question rather than closing the line down: confirm the status.

`model_eligible_wide_interval` is the one I would most like your view on. An erratic line can be forecast, but the point number is close to useless and the value sits in the interval, which drives safety stock rather than the plan. Splitting it from `model_eligible` says that out loud. Folding the two together would be simpler and less honest.

## 2. The table

Five demand classes by three quality bands. Fifteen cells, and one exception that sits outside them.

| | Quality clean | Quality caveated | Quality not usable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **smooth** | `model_eligible` | `model_eligible` + caveat | `refused_data_quality` |
| **erratic** | `model_eligible_wide_interval` | same + caveat | `refused_data_quality` |
| **intermittent** | `intermittent_methods` | same + caveat | `refused_data_quality` |
| **lumpy** | `policy_only` | same + caveat | `refused_data_quality` |
| **unclassifiable** | `insufficient_evidence` | `insufficient_evidence` | `refused_data_quality` |

**Discontinuation sits outside this table**, because it applies whatever the class and whatever the band. See the precedence order below.

The rules that make it work:

**A caveated band does not change the decision.** It attaches to it. The caveat is named in the reason and shown on the row, and the client decides whether to accept it. Changing the route on a caveat would mean building a finding-by-finding decision table, which is real work and which we deliberately deferred.

**Not usable overrides everything, including the demand class.** Fixing the data comes before any judgement about demand, so `refused_data_quality` wins even on a line that classified perfectly well.

**Precedence, in order, added in 1.1.**

1. **Discontinued wins over everything.** Six or more periods with no demand, whatever the class, whatever the band. The status question comes before the data question, because a clean extract of a dead line is still a dead line.
2. **Not usable comes next**, producing `refused_data_quality`.
3. **Otherwise the class table above applies**, including unclassifiable to `insufficient_evidence`.

Routing reads the `SERIES_DISCONTINUED` finding from `quality_result`. It does not recompute the trailing gap. One implementation of any judgement, and the quality engine already owns this one.

**Stale is not discontinued.** Three to five trailing periods raises staleness, which is a caveat and leaves the route unchanged. Only six or more triggers the exception. RTG-60602 in fixture 31 sits at four periods precisely to hold that line.

**ABC volume class does not affect the decision at all.** A lumpy A line and a lumpy C line get the same answer, because the maths is the same. Volume decides what a planner looks at first, not what the tool concludes. Keeping those apart is what stops the tool quietly recommending different treatment for big customers.

## 3. What each line carries

Four fields:

- `decision`, one of the seven above
- `reason`, one sentence in plain language, naming the numbers that drove it
- `forecast_eligible`, true or false
- `refusal`, null unless refused, otherwise a code and **what would change the answer**

That last part matters. A refusal with no route forward reads as the tool giving up. Every refusal names the thing that would unblock it. It is the resolution pattern from story 1.1 applied to routing.

---

## 3.1 Resolutions are chosen, not typed

You are right that this is an input, and an input needs a vocabulary.

My earlier wording, "confirmation from the business that the line is live or dead", described what a planner would say out loud. That is fine as a prompt and useless as a field. Free text cannot be tested, cannot be counted across a portfolio, cannot drive a re-run, and turns the client's answer into something a consultant has to read and interpret rather than something the tool acts on.

So every refusal carries a **closed list of resolution codes**. The planner picks one. A free-text note may sit beside it, but nothing in the system depends on the note, and the note is client data so it lives in the bundle and never in the manifest.

### `discontinued_confirm_status`

| Code | What it means | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| `DISCONTINUED_CONFIRMED` | The line is finished. | Excluded from forecast scope, and flagged for obsolete stock and master data review. A dead SKU still holding safety stock is a working capital finding. |
| `SUPERSEDED_BY_SKU` | Replaced by another part number. | Requires a successor, **chosen from the SKUs in the uploaded file** rather than typed. History should be chained to the successor before anything is forecast. |
| `STILL_ACTIVE_DEMAND_GAP` | The product is live and the gap is real: an outage, a lost customer, an allocation. | History is usable, and the gap is treated as an event rather than as a pattern. |
| `STILL_ACTIVE_DATA_MISSING` | The product is live and the demand is missing from this extract. | The extract is wrong. Nothing about this line is trustworthy until it is re-supplied. |
| `DEFER` | No decision yet. | The line stays refused and appears on the open items list. |

`SUPERSEDED_BY_SKU` is the one I would expect to be picked most often on a packaging portfolio, and it is the reason this cannot be a yes or no question. A part number that dies because artwork or a pack size changed is not a discontinued product, and its history belongs to its successor.

### `refused_data_quality`

| Code | What it means |
|---|---|
| `SUPPLY_LONGER_HISTORY` | Re-extract with more periods. |
| `SUPPLY_CORRECTED_EXTRACT` | The data is wrong rather than short. |
| `TREAT_AS_NEW_LINE` | There genuinely is no history. Forecast it as a launch, by analogue and judgement, not from its own past. |
| `EXCLUDE_FROM_SCOPE` | Out of scope for this engagement. |
| `DEFER` | No decision yet. |

`TREAT_AS_NEW_LINE` is the honest answer for RTG-60401, the five period launch. Accepting a statistical forecast on five points would be the dishonest one, and I would rather the tool refuse and offer the launch route than quietly proceed.

### `insufficient_evidence`

`SUPPLY_LONGER_HISTORY`, `TREAT_AS_NEW_LINE`, `EXCLUDE_FROM_SCOPE`, `DEFER`.

### Rules

- The code is required. The note is optional and carries no behaviour.
- A resolution produces a **new pass**, recorded in the manifest options, exactly as story 1.1 does for validation. It is not an override: the client is supplying information the tool did not have, not disagreeing with it.
- Resolution codes are a closed vocabulary and are covered by the cross-stage test. No code may mean two things.
- Unresolved lines are not a failure state. `DEFER` is a legitimate answer, and the open items list is a deliverable in its own right.

**Question for the build session, with a default.** Story 1.1 already implements resolution passes for validation. If that mechanism generalises cheaply, 2.2 captures resolutions and re-runs. If it does not, 2.2 defines and displays the vocabulary and records a supplied code, and the capture and re-run move to 2.3. Default to the second if it is at all close, because the vocabulary is the part I need fixed now.

## 4. One thing that needs your decision

Your implementation default 4 said quality bands are never duplicated inside another result. Routing breaks that, and I think it should.

A routing decision **depends** on the quality band, so the band has to be recorded next to the decision as `quality_band_at_decision`. Otherwise a bundle opened in six months shows a refusal with no evidence of what caused it, and the decision is not reproducible.

That is provenance rather than duplication: it records the input to a judgement at the moment the judgement was made. The quality stage still owns the band. Routing owns a snapshot of what it saw.

I recommend allowing it, scoped exactly to that one field.
