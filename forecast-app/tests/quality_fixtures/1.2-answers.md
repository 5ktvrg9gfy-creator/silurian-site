Authoritative. The contract in `1.2-contract.md` is accepted as written, with two additions noted under Q5 and Q7.

I ran every proposed threshold against the fixture data before answering. One default is wrong and would misclassify a SKU. Two need a discriminator that was not specified. The rest hold, and the agreed values are now in `expected_quality.json` under `thresholds`, so the implementation and the tests read from one source.

## Q1. Rules for `caveated` and `not_usable` per SKU

**Decision: your default is right in principle. Here are the explicit criteria, plus one addition.**

`clean`, no findings that limit a conclusion.

`caveated`, usable with a named limitation. Covers outlier candidates, level shifts, suspect zeros, series staleness, discontinuation and short history down to the not-usable floor.

`not_usable`, any one of:

- span shorter than 6 periods
- coverage below 50 percent
- trailing gap of 12 periods or more

**The addition: `not_usable` carries a `resolvable` flag.** Some series are unusable because information is missing, and some because time is. This mirrors the resolution concept from story 1.1 and it changes what the client is asked to do.

- `PKG-30220` is `not_usable` with `resolvable: true`. Coverage is 24 percent only because zero months were omitted from the export. Confirm that missing means zero and it becomes a perfectly good intermittent series. The action is a question to the client.
- `PKG-40001` is `not_usable` with `resolvable: false`. Five periods of history. Nothing the client can supply fixes that. The report must say "too new to assess" rather than anything that reads as criticism of their data, because this is a live product doing nothing wrong.

**Discontinued series stay `caveated`, not `not_usable`.** The history is complete and correct, so the data is not the problem. The implication line does the work: history is sound, the line appears discontinued, exclude it from forward-looking claims unless the client confirms it is active. Do not add a fourth band for relevance.

**Immateriality never changes the band.** `PKG-40006` has complete, clean data and 0.01 percent of volume. It is `clean` and carries a `VOLUME_IMMATERIAL` finding. Findings and bands are related but not the same thing, and conflating them would let a materiality judgement masquerade as a data quality one.

## Q2. Thresholds for short, stale and discontinued

**Decision: the values are right. The measurement basis for short history is wrong and will misclassify a SKU in your own fixture.**

Short history must be measured on **span**, first period to last period, never on **rows present**.

Tested against fixture 20: `PKG-30220` has 8 rows across a 33 period span, because zero months were omitted. Counting rows makes it a new product. Counting span makes it what it is, a sparse three year series. It is the difference between "this line is too new to assess" and "we need to know whether your blanks mean zero", which are opposite conversations to have with a client.

Rows present measures sparsity. Span measures age. Keep them apart.

Agreed values, now in `expected_quality.json`:

| Threshold | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Short history | fewer than 18 periods | span |
| Not usable, too short | fewer than 6 periods | span |
| Not usable, too sparse | coverage below 50 percent | rows over span |
| Series stale | 3 to 5 periods behind the portfolio cut-off | trailing gap |
| Series discontinued | 6 or more periods behind | trailing gap |
| Extract stale | portfolio cut-off 2 or more periods before `as_of_date` | trailing gap |

Two points on the staleness set. Measuring series staleness against the **portfolio cut-off** rather than `as_of_date` is right and I want to flag that you got it right, because it is what makes fixture 21 behave: when every series ends together, no series is stale relative to its peers, and the finding belongs to the extract. The extract-level threshold of 2 was not in your list, so I have set it. One period behind is normal for a monthly extract taken after month end, two or more is a stale file.

Verified on the fixtures: `PKG-40005` trailing 5 gives `SERIES_STALE`. `PKG-40002` trailing 9 gives `SERIES_DISCONTINUED`. Fixture 21 at trailing 4 across all four series gives one `EXTRACT_STALE` and no series findings. Control at trailing 0 gives nothing.

## Q3. `VOLUME_IMMATERIAL` threshold

**Decision: keep 0.1 percent as the flag. Add a portfolio-level long tail statement, because the individual flag does not answer the question a client actually asks.**

Tested on fixture 20, 0.1 percent catches exactly one SKU, `PKG-40006` at 0.01 percent. That is correct as a per-SKU statement, and on its own it is nearly useless in a report.

The useful statement is cumulative. On fixture 20 the top three SKUs carry 83.4 percent of volume, and four SKUs sit beyond the 99 percent cumulative line carrying 0.73 percent between them. "Four of your twelve lines are together under one percent of volume" is a sentence a client acts on. "One line is 0.01 percent" is not.

So report both: the per-SKU flag at 0.1 percent, and a portfolio finding naming the SKUs beyond 99 percent cumulative volume and their combined share. Record the threshold and the weighting basis in the report, as you proposed.

## Q4. Distinguishing `GAP_BLOCK` from a sparse series

**Decision: your instinct is right, and a coverage threshold is the wrong discriminator. Use gap concentration instead.**

The question is not how much is missing, it is whether the missing periods sit in one run. One contiguous block is an event, usually a system migration or a site transfer. Scattered singles are sparsity.

```
GAP_BLOCK when longest_gap >= 3 and longest_gap / gap_count >= 0.8
ZERO_VS_MISSING_AMBIGUOUS when coverage < 70 percent and the above does not hold
```

Verified on fixture 20:

- `PKG-40003`, gap count 4, longest gap 4, ratio 1.0, coverage 88.6 percent. `GAP_BLOCK`. Correct, it is the planted migration gap.
- `PKG-30220`, gap count 25, longest gap 9, ratio 0.36, coverage 24.2 percent. `ZERO_VS_MISSING_AMBIGUOUS`. Correct, it is the omitted zeros case.

A coverage-only rule gets `PKG-40003` right by luck and would break on a series with a long block and low coverage.

## Q5. Deriving the portfolio band

**Decision: your volume rule is correct and incomplete. It returns the wrong answer on fixture 21, which is the file built to test exactly this.**

Your rule: `not_usable` at 20 percent of volume, `caveated` at 5 percent, otherwise clean.

On fixture 21 every SKU is individually clean, because none is stale relative to the portfolio cut-off. Non-clean volume is zero, so your rule returns `clean` for a file that is four months out of date. That is the one outcome the fixture exists to prevent.

**The portfolio band is the worse of two things:**

1. The volume-weighted SKU rule, as you specified it.
2. Any portfolio-level finding, which caps the band at `caveated`.

A portfolio finding never forces `not_usable` on its own, because an extract being stale does not make the data wrong, only old.

Verified across the set: fixture 20 gives `caveated` at 16.4 percent flagged volume. Fixture 22 gives `caveated` at 56 percent. Fixture 21 gives `caveated` through the portfolio finding. Control gives `clean`.

Always show both shares, as you said. On fixture 20 they are 67 percent of SKUs and 16.4 percent of volume, and that gap is the report's strongest sentence.

## Q6. Source and precedence for `as_of_date`

**Decision: accepted exactly as proposed, with one field added.**

Explicit analysis date from the user, defaulting to the server date, immutable within the run record, fixture overrides through the same field.

Add a flag recording whether the date was user-supplied or server-defaulted. When a client asks why the same file banded differently in June and September, the answer is in that one field, and without it you are reconstructing the reason from timestamps.

## Q7. Grain override in the UI

**Decision: accepted, with two additions.**

Show the inferred grain in the report with its evidence, not just the conclusion. Modal spacing, and how many series agree with it. "Monthly, inferred from 12 of 12 series" survives a challenge from a planner who runs a 4-4-5 calendar. "Monthly" on its own does not.

**The addition that matters: detect inconsistent grain across SKUs.** Nothing in story 1.1 checks it, and nothing in your contract catches it. A file with weekly rows for some SKUs and monthly for others computes coverage and gap metrics that are quietly meaningless for every series. Add `GRAIN_INCONSISTENT` as a portfolio finding when series disagree on modal spacing, naming the minority group.

This is a genuine hole rather than a preference. Tell me if you want a fixture for it and I will build one. It is not in the current set because I did not anticipate it either.

## Contract review

`1.2-contract.md` is accepted as written. Three things in it are better than what my brief specified, and I am adopting them:

- The pure function signature taking a `ValidationResult` rather than anything file-shaped. It makes the boundary unarguable.
- Dropping severity from quality findings entirely and replacing it with `implication` and `action`. Severity was a validation concept and it had no business here.

One correction to the detector order in section 46 of the contract. Step 9 computes short history and immateriality last, but step 10 assigns bands. Short history is a band input, so it has to be complete before banding, which your order does satisfy. No change needed, I checked it rather than assumed it.
